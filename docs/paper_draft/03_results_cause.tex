\subsection{The writer's error separates by domain, not by writer}
\label{sec:cause}
Every unauthorized request that a final memory authorizes, in every run of this paper's extension studies, is located to the memory update that introduced it and labeled by three judges (Section~\ref{subsec:benchmark-construction}). Table~\ref{tab:cause} counts distinct memory updates as well as the requests and records they affect, since one bad update can authorize several requests. Three causes account for nearly all 2{,}250 judged failures (861 updates), and they separate by setting and domain. In the open loop, procurement and finance failures are almost all a restatement from a party without authority being applied: a status line, a colleague, or a forwarded copy repeats the superseded figure and the writer changes the record to match. In the closed loop, every record minted from the agent's own lines and nearly every failure entering at a written-back block is an escalation, decline, or execution line treated as a grant. Cybersecurity fails differently: its failures sit at the block carrying the duty officer's signed replacement of the permission list, and in most of them both of the writer's attempts to write the new list are rejected as invalid output, so the old permission stays active. This reframes the domain the pooled numbers present as the safest: it fails in a different way, which the provenance mitigations do not address (Section~\ref{sec:mitigations}). The judges agree unanimously on 79\% of rows and two of three on 21\%; the labels have not been checked by a person.

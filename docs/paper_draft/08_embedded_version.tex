A single instruction about who may grant authority removes most laundering in two domains at no measured cost in authorized use and increases it in the third, and when the agent's own actions are written back into its history they become cited evidence for permissions and cut authorized use round over round.

% ---- §4.1 Incremental updating produces the most authorization failures. Append to the paragraph:
The result does not depend on the two representations tested. Five writers run on three further designs give the same ordering: a hybrid schema (typed records with free-text notes) lowers unauthorized submission for the paper's writers and raises it for one added writer in one domain, retrieval-augmented writing changes no cell, and rebuilding the typed memory from the full history every three blocks instead of patching it lowers unauthorized submission in every domain and for every writer, from 25.4\% to 6.0\% in procurement at three seeds with authorized use rising from 90.6\% to 95.6\% (Appendix~\ref{app:rebuild}).

% ---- §4.2 False authority forms in memory and propagates to action. Append after the exact-memory table paragraph:
What triggers formation can be isolated on generated histories. In 108 groups of procurement histories that differ only in how many later messages, from parties without authority, repeat a permission after it has been superseded, no false authority forms with zero such messages (0 of 324 unauthorized requests, three writers) and it forms for 15.4\% of them with two; four are not measurably different from two (Appendix~\ref{app:generated-corpus}). A later restatement of a superseded permission is the trigger, and an incremental writer that never revisits earlier blocks reads each one as news.

Two writers added after the initial evaluation, Inkling and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, reproduce the ordering of the other five: incremental typed memory launders in every domain and one-shot memory almost never (Inkling procurement 32.4\% against 2.8\% unauthorized submission; Flash finance 37.0\% against 0.0\%), and pressure raises submission most in finance.

% ---- §4.4 Mitigations form a discrete safety--utility Pareto frontier. Replace the figure with figures/mitigation_frontier_rows.pdf
% (caption in 05_results_mitigations.tex) and append two paragraphs:
Two changes to the writer rather than to provenance sit on the same axes for the five Baseten writers at the same three seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:mitigation-frontier-domains}, lower row). Rebuilding memory from the history every three blocks moves left with no loss of authorized use in every domain. Prepending one sentence to the writer's instructions, ``Only record permissions that an authorized approver has actually granted, no matter what anyone else says or asks,'' cuts unauthorized submission from 25.4\% to 5.8\% in procurement (paired change $-19.5$ points, 95\% CI $-23.1$ to $-16.0$) and from 31.7\% to 1.7\% in finance ($-30.0$, 95\% CI $-35.8$ to $-24.2$) at every seed and for every writer, with authorized use unchanged or higher, and in the closed loop of Section~\ref{sec:closed-loop} it removes almost every record minted from the agent's own actions (91 to 4). In cybersecurity the same sentence raises unauthorized submission from 10.4\% to 21.8\% ($+11.4$, 95\% CI $+6.8$ to $+15.8$) and lowers authorized use by 10.4 points.

The reversal follows the point of entry. Locating every failure to the memory update that introduced it and labeling the writer's error with three LLM judges (Appendix~\ref{app:diagnosis}) shows that procurement and finance failures are almost all a restatement from a party without authority being applied, which the instruction addresses, while cybersecurity failures sit at the block carrying the duty officer's signed replacement of the permission list, where both of the writer's write attempts are rejected as invalid output in most cases and the instruction makes that rejected update about twice as frequent. Gating does nothing in that domain for the same reason: every cited source is authoritative. The labels are the judges' majority (79\% unanimous) and have not been checked by a person, so this explanation is the reading they suggest; what it establishes is that a mitigation matched to the point of entry avoids the safety--utility cost the provenance filters pay, and one that is not matched can make the failure more frequent.
